# AI adoption strategy for a cybersecurity operations scenario

**Context:** Individual Module 3 assignment in *AI in Practice* for the 2025 Diploma in AI for Business. The assessment brief explicitly allowed a hypothetical organization and asked for a strategy, value and impact criteria, and a method for weighing trade-offs. My response names a cybersecurity organization, “Molex Cyber.” This public summary treats it as an academic scenario and does not imply a client engagement.

## Problem

The scenario considers delivery delays and resource planning in a cybersecurity operation. Leaders must choose between extending existing vendor tools and building an internal AI-assisted planning and governance capability while protecting customer trust.

## My approach

I first distinguished AI innovation management from gradual process improvement and identified barriers including data readiness, governance, trust, and organizational culture. For the application question, I compared an extension of existing vendor tools with an internally developed AI-assisted planning and governance capability. I considered strategic fit, delivery feasibility, human adoption, oversight, risk, and expected business value, then explained the trade-offs behind my recommendation. The scenario covered predictive sprint planning, resource allocation, compliance review, vendor dependence, and phased adoption.

The numerical budget, efficiency, and market-share figures in the assignment are scenario estimates. They are **not measured results**. A real investment decision would require validated baselines, sensitivity analysis, pilot testing, total cost, security review, and evidence that staff actually use the system.

## Portfolio takeaway

The work demonstrates a way to connect AI adoption to process redesign and assurance. The decision is not only whether to build or buy a model; it includes who owns its recommendations, how its output is checked, and what evidence would justify expansion.
