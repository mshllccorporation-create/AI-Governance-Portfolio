# Applied organizational AI governance assessments | anonymized TRAILS experience

**Context:** NSF-funded TRAILS / AI-GEA research and assessment work  
**Public description:** Cohort-level summary; participating organizations are intentionally anonymous.

## What I did

I conducted and applied an evidence-based AI governance and explainability assessment methodology with participating organizations across different AI maturity levels and operating contexts. The work moved beyond framework design into live client-facing assessment activity, including authorized evidence review, governance-gap analysis, explainability and accountability review, evidence-readiness analysis, and organization-specific recommendations.

The deliverables included organization-level assessment reports and coaching or implementation plans. The coaching work translated findings into practical steps for inventory, accountable ownership, human-in-the-loop controls, approval records, explainability artifacts, evidence ledgers, monitoring, reconstruction exercises, contestability, and evidence packs.

## Assessment areas

Depending on the organization and scope, the reviews considered:

- AI inventory, use cases, ownership, and decision authority;
- governance policies, oversight, and operational controls;
- bias and drift monitoring;
- explainability, transparency, and human oversight;
- evidence reporting, traceability, and auditability;
- contestability, correction, and appeal pathways;
- data boundaries, privacy, and third-party dependencies;
- safe-use constraints, risk management, and escalation; and
- evidence readiness and practical improvement priorities.

## Evidence-first method

The assessment standard was deliberately evidence-led:

`Collect organizational evidence → assess governance questions → distinguish assertion from evidence → identify gaps → constrain authority where necessary → specify required controls → create an implementation plan → build toward evidence-backed governance`

Unsupported controls remained unsupported. Proposed controls were identified as proposed or to be implemented rather than presented as completed capability.

## How the public version protects participants

The portfolio does not identify participating organizations or reproduce their reports. Public examples use neutral labels such as **Organization A**, **Organization B**, and **Organization C**. Any separately illustrative example is labeled as such and is not presented as an assessment result. The public version excludes:

- organization names, logos, and identifying URLs;
- participant names and contact information;
- organization-specific scores, findings, quotations, or operational details;
- confidential source documents and internal working papers;
- complete assessment instruments and proprietary control logic; and
- dates or combinations of facts that could reasonably re-identify a participant.
